WHO ACTUALLY OWNS THIS RISK?™

Executive Security Risk Governance Diagnostic™

A security risk can be clearly identified and still leave an organization unsure about who owns it, who has authority to act, or who is accountable for the outcome.

This Diagnostic examines that relationship around one live business or security risk. Its purpose is to establish whether risk ownership, decision authority, accountability and relevant treatment responsibility are appropriately connected, based on the information available.

One real risk. One governance question. One defined result.

CORE PRINCIPLE
Accountability without corresponding authority is a governance problem.
This is a focused diagnostic. It is not a security assessment, risk register, governance audit or decision-making service. Record what the available information establishes. Where the position cannot be established, record UNCLEAR rather than filling the gap with an assumption.

Instructions

How to Use the Diagnostic

Work on one live risk and one related business decision, activity or change. Record the governance arrangement as it currently operates. Do not redesign the arrangement or attempt to resolve the governance issue while completing the Diagnostic.

PHASE 1 | TRIAGE
Define the decision and identify the governance relationship.
PHASE 2 | STRESS-TEST
Examine how the relationship operates and locate any material gap.
PHASE 3 | CLASSIFY
State the governance condition and classify the overall position.

Underlying logic: DEFINE → IDENTIFY → TEST → LOCATE → STATE → CLASSIFY → INTERPRET

Phase 1 · Triage

01 | Define the Decision

Start with the business situation that gives the risk its significance.

Validation gate
  • A specific business decision, activity or change has been identified.
  • A specific risk has been identified.
  • The relationship between the business situation and the risk can be explained.

If any item cannot be established, record the uncertainty. Do not proceed on the basis of an assumed relationship.

Phase 1 · Triage

02 | Identify the Governance Relationship

Identify the roles or functions involved in the current arrangement. A role may be held by an individual, business function, committee or other authorized body. The same person may hold more than one role.

Position definitions
ESTABLISHED: Available information supports the existence of the role, mandate or authority and its relevance to the matter examined.
NOT ESTABLISHED: Available information indicates that the role, mandate or authority has not been assigned, defined or provided for in the current arrangement.
UNCLEAR: Available information is insufficient to determine whether the role, mandate or authority exists or how it operates.
Do not record NOT ESTABLISHED merely because supporting documentation is unavailable. Where the actual arrangement cannot be determined, record UNCLEAR.

Role integrity

The roles above do not have to belong to the same person or function. The important question is whether the relationships between them work in practice.

AUTHORITY → DELEGATION → ESCALATION → ACCOUNTABILITY → ACTION

A Risk Owner does not necessarily have authority to approve the underlying business decision. Security or Risk may provide advice without holding approval authority or authority to accept residual risk. Do not treat a role as established merely because it appears in an organizational chart or policy. Consider whether the mandate and its connection to the decision can be established from the available information.

Phase 2 · Stress-test

03 | Test the Governance Connection

Answer each question based on the current operational arrangement, not the intended governance model. Use NOT APPLICABLE only where the question genuinely does not apply.

Critical governance controls
  • Access to the relevant decision authority.
  • An effective escalation route.
  • Authority to accept residual risk, where applicable.
  • Connection between business accountability and decision authority.
  • The ability to act on agreed treatment.
  • Integration of relevant Security/Risk information into the decision process.

Record UNCLEAR where the position cannot be established. A missing formal document does not automatically prove that an arrangement is ineffective. An assumed or unverified arrangement should not be treated as established.

Distinguishing related questions

Questions 1 and 6 test different relationships. Question 1 examines whether the Risk Owner can challenge, influence or escalate a material decision. Question 6 examines whether the responsible role can influence the activity that creates or materially changes the risk.

Questions 3 and 4 also differ. Question 3 examines whether an effective escalation mechanism exists; Question 4 examines whether it provides access to the relevant decision authority. A documented route does not, by itself, demonstrate that escalation works in practice.

DIAGNOSTIC RULE
Do not calculate a score from the number of YES or NO responses. A single material disconnect can establish the principal governance condition.

An unanswered question is not evidence of a governance failure. An uncertain finding must remain uncertain until sufficient information is available.

Phase 2 · Stress-test

04 | Locate the Principal Governance Condition

Ownership Gap

Responsibility for the risk has not been clearly assigned or cannot be established.

Authority Gap

The responsible role does not have access to the authority required to influence a material decision affecting the risk.

Accountability Gap

Accountability for the affected business outcome is not appropriately connected to the authority governing the relevant decision.

Residual-Risk Acceptance Gap

The authority to accept or retain relevant residual risk is unclear, absent or disconnected from the governance arrangement.

Treatment Responsibility Gap

Treatment responsibility is not connected to the authority, resources or ability required to act.

Escalation Gap

An effective route for escalating a material unresolved issue to the appropriate authority has not been established.

Decision-Integration Gap

Relevant Security/Risk information cannot reliably reach or be considered by the authorized decision-maker.

No Material Governance Gap Identified

The available information indicates that relevant ownership, authority, accountability and action relationships are sufficiently connected for the matter examined.

Where evidence supports more than one condition, identify the principal condition and record additional material conditions. If information is insufficient to establish the principal condition, record UNCLEAR and consider whether the overall classification must be UNDEFINED.

Phase 2 · Stress-test

05 | State the Governance Condition

Summarize the relationship established by the Diagnostic. Use language that reflects the available information; do not convert an assumption into a finding.

Phase 3 · Classify

06 | Classify the Governance Position

ALIGNED

Risk ownership, material decision authority, accountability and relevant treatment responsibility are sufficiently connected. This does not authorize the activity or establish that the risk is acceptable.

CONSTRAINED

Risk ownership and a workable connection to decision authority are established, but a material limitation restricts the responsible role's ability to influence or act.

MISALIGNED

A material responsibility and the authority required to influence the risk are separated, and no effective mechanism adequately connects them.

UNDEFINED

The available information does not establish with sufficient clarity who owns the risk, who has authority, who is accountable or how the roles connect.

Classification decision rule

  1. UNDEFINED: Insufficient information prevents a reliable determination of a material governance relationship.
  2. MISALIGNED: A material responsibility-authority connection is absent or ineffective and no adequate mechanism connects the roles.
  3. CONSTRAINED: The connection is established but a material limitation restricts the ability to influence or act.
  4. ALIGNED: Relevant governance relationships are established and sufficiently effective for the matter examined.

This is a decision framework, not a numerical scoring model. An UNCLEAR response does not automatically override an otherwise established relationship; the uncertainty must relate to a material relationship needed to determine the overall position. Where material findings conflict and the conflict cannot be resolved, record the uncertainty and consider UNDEFINED.

Classification control
  • Is risk ownership established?
  • Is authority over the material decision established?
  • Do delegation and escalation provide a workable connection to that authority?
  • Is business accountability connected to the decision?
  • Is treatment responsibility supported by the authority and ability required to act?
  • Can relevant Security/Risk information inform the decision?
  • Does material uncertainty prevent a reliable conclusion?
