Interpreting the Executive Security Risk Governance Diagnostic™ and Selecting an Appropriate Response

Clarifying the connection between security risk, decision authority, accountability and action.

How to Use This Guide

Complete sections in order after finishing Tool 1.

03

Interpret the Diagnostic Result

Classification informs the response; it does not determine the business outcome.

ALIGNED | CONTINUE

The relevant governance relationships appear established and sufficiently effective for the matter examined. Confirm that arrangements remain appropriate as risk, operating conditions and decision requirements evolve. ALIGNED does not mean the risk is eliminated, controls always work, or no further action is required. If no consequential decision remains unresolved, the organization may continue through existing processes.

CONSTRAINED | ESCALATE

The relationships appear to exist, but a material limitation restricts the ability of a responsible person or function to influence, authorize or implement necessary action. Identify the limitation and the person or body authorized to resolve it. Escalation does not transfer risk ownership to Security or Risk.

MISALIGNED | RECONNECT

A material connection between responsibility, authority, accountability or decision-making appears absent or ineffective. Determine which relationship requires clarification or correction. Establish who is responsible, who has authority to decide, who must act and how unresolved matters will be escalated. This concerns the evidenced governance arrangement; it does not by itself establish individual failure.

UNDEFINED | ESTABLISH

Information is insufficient to determine whether a material governance relationship exists or operates as required. Identify missing information, mandates, decisions or confirmations. Do not classify a relationship as absent solely because a document is unavailable. Establish material facts before choosing a more specific response.

05

Identify the Appropriate Response Path

Select one path supported by the evidence.

PATH A | GOVERNANCE CONDITION ONLY

Use where a governance condition needs attention but no specific consequential decision remains unresolved. Continue through existing governance, management, escalation or corrective-action processes. Clarify roles and authority as needed, and record action, accountable role and review date. Consider advisory support only if existing arrangements cannot resolve the matter.

PATH B | CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION REMAINS UNRESOLVED

Use where a specific consequential decision remains unresolved and sufficient information exists to examine its requirements and relevant considerations. The Executive Security Decision Review™ may be appropriate. It examines one decision, relevant risk considerations, available options and issues requiring clarification. It does not make the decision, assume risk ownership or confer authority. It requires separate qualification, acceptance and commissioning.

PATH C | GOVERNANCE ARRANGEMENT MAY BE PREVENTING RESOLUTION

Where a governance issue materially obstructs an unresolved decision, consider the Executive Security Decision Review™ first if decision requirements, considerations or options are not sufficiently established. Where requirements are established and the principal barrier concerns unresolved ownership, authority, accountability or their connection, qualification for the Executive Decision Sprint™ may be considered directly. Separate qualification, acceptance and commissioning are required.

PATH D | INFORMATION INSUFFICIENT

Use where information does not reliably establish the governance condition, decision status or relationship. Identify missing information about the decision, authorized decision-maker, Risk Owner, mandate, status or escalation arrangements. Determine whether existing processes can obtain it before considering a service.

Paths identify possible responses. They do not automatically commission or qualify an organization for an advisory service.

06

Decision Resolution Check

Answer each question using available evidence.

Interpretation:
  • If no consequential decision remains unresolved, consider Path A.
  • If an unresolved decision can be examined with sufficient information, consider Path B.
  • If governance materially obstructs an unresolved decision, consider Path C, but first assess whether the decision itself is sufficiently established.
  • If material facts remain unknown, consider Path D.
Where decision clarity and governance are both barriers, do not assume governance can be addressed effectively before decision requirements are understood. Record conflicting answers rather than forcing a conclusion.
07

Decision Integration Check

Assess whether relevant Security or Risk information reaches the authorized decision process.

A decision-integration problem does not mean advice must be accepted, a decision reversed or Security/Risk given decision authority. Response depends on decision status, governance arrangements and the nature of the constraint.

08

Keep Responsibility and Authority Distinct

Identify the relevant role, then record whether its assignment and mandate are established.

The same person may hold more than one role. Record each role separately because the responsibilities are different, even when one person performs them. Enter the role or function, rather than a person's name, unless the name is needed to identify the arrangement.

How to complete each role entry
  • ESTABLISHED: Available evidence supports that the role or mechanism is assigned or provided for, and its relevant responsibility, mandate or authority is sufficiently clear.
  • NOT ESTABLISHED: Available evidence indicates that the role or mechanism is unassigned, undefined or not provided for in the arrangement being examined.
  • UNCLEAR: There is not enough information to determine whether the role or mechanism exists, is assigned or operates as required.

Important: Missing or incomplete documentation alone does not prove that a role is NOT ESTABLISHED. If you cannot determine the position from the evidence available, select UNCLEAR. Do not guess.

The person or body authorized under the organization's arrangements to make or approve the underlying business decision.

The person or function responsible for the business objective, asset, operation or activity affected by the risk.

The person or role assigned responsibility for managing the identified risk within the organization's arrangements. This does not automatically include authority over the underlying business decision.

The person or body with the mandate to approve, reject or otherwise determine the specific consequential decision examined. This field focuses on authority for this decision, not general seniority.

The person or body authorized to accept residual risk where the organization's policy or decision framework requires formal acceptance. If formal acceptance is not applicable to this matter, state that in the notes.

The person or function responsible for implementing the agreed treatment, controls or corrective action. This may differ from both the Risk Owner and the decision authority.

The person or function that identifies, analyses, explains or advises on risk and possible responses within its mandate. Providing advice does not, by itself, confer decision authority.

The role accountable for the relevant business outcome, activity or decision under the organization's governance arrangements. Accountability for an outcome is not the same as authority to approve the decision.

The person, committee or established process through which an issue that cannot be resolved at the current level reaches an appropriate decision-maker.

Governance principle: Identifying a risk does not confer decision authority. Advice does not automatically transfer risk ownership. Accountability without corresponding authority or a workable escalation mechanism may create a governance problem.

When Further Examination May Be Appropriate

Advisory services require separate consideration.

Executive Security Decision Review™

May be appropriate where a specific consequential decision remains unresolved, its requirements and relevant considerations can be examined, options or consequences require structured analysis, or governance issues may affect the decision. Executive Decision Sprint™

May be considered where decision requirements are sufficiently established and a material governance arrangement involving ownership, authority, accountability or their connection prevents resolution. Existing organizational process

Further advisory engagement may not be necessary where existing arrangements can resolve the matter.

11

Governance Response Principles

Apply these principles throughout.

  1. Evidence before classification. Separate established facts from assumptions and questions.
  2. Responsibility does not automatically confer authority. Risk management responsibility may not include business decision authority.
  3. Advice does not transfer accountability. The authorized business authority remains responsible for assigned decisions.
  4. Escalation should connect the issue to authority. Unresolved matters should reach an appropriate decision-maker.
  5. Missing information is not proof of failure. An unavailable document does not alone prove a relationship does not exist.
  6. A governance finding does not dictate the business outcome. The authorized decision-maker determines the business response.
  7. Advisory services require separate consideration. A classification or path does not commission an engagement.
  8. Address the actual barrier. Examine decision clarity and governance in an appropriate sequence.

Governing Principle

The central proposition behind the Diagnostic and Guide.

A risk can be identified without the authority to resolve it.

Effective risk governance connects responsibility, decision authority, accountability and action. This Guide helps identify where that connection is established, constrained, misaligned or not yet established.
