The governance problem

When Identifying the Risk Is Not Enough

A security concern can be documented and escalated without resolving who has the authority to act.

SITUATION 01

The Disconnected Owner

A risk owner is responsible for an exposure but cannot authorise the budget, operational change or other action needed to address it.

SITUATION 02

The Unconnected Authority

A business executive can approve an activity, but the relationship between that authority, risk ownership and accountability is unclear.

SITUATION 03

The Isolated Security Function

Security identifies a significant concern but cannot approve the activity, direct the business response or accept residual risk on the organisation's behalf.

The Diagnostic examines these relationships without transferring business responsibility to the security function.

The result

The Four Governance Classifications

The Diagnostic uses four classifications to describe the relationship between risk ownership, decision authority and accountability.

ALIGNED

The relevant responsibilities and decision rights are sufficiently connected within the information examined.

CONSTRAINED

Risk ownership is established, but the authority required to act is limited.

MISALIGNED

Risk responsibility and the authority needed to influence the decision are not effectively connected.

UNDEFINED

The available information does not establish the relevant ownership, authority or accountability clearly enough.

These classifications describe governance conditions, not the severity or acceptability of the underlying risk. The paid Diagnostic applies the framework to one live risk. Its accompanying Decision Guide explains how to interpret the result and consider the appropriate next step.

Scope

What the Diagnostic Examines

The examination focuses on the relationship between the responsibilities and decision rights involved in one live risk.

  • Risk ownership
  • Decision authority
  • Accountability
  • Treatment responsibility
  • Residual-risk decisions
  • Escalation
  • Security and Risk input

Purpose

What the Result Means

These responsibilities may sit with different people or functions. The question is whether their relationship provides a clear basis for action through the organisation's established governance arrangements.

The Diagnostic does not determine who should own the risk, grant authority or prescribe the business outcome.

The detailed diagnostic questions, classification rules, treatment of incomplete information and interpretation procedures are contained in the paid instrument and accompanying guide.

Limitations

Scope and Professional Boundaries

The Diagnostic is a self-guided instrument. It is not:

  • A comprehensive security risk assessment
  • An audit or assurance engagement
  • A governance redesign
  • A risk appetite determination
  • A compliance certification
  • Legal or regulatory advice
  • Specialist technical advice
  • An implementation engagement
  • A business decision-making service

TopLink does not make the business decision, accept risk on the organisation's behalf, assume risk ownership or decision authority, authorise expenditure or operational activity, or implement the resulting course of action through this product.

The instrument relies on information entered by the person completing it. It does not independently verify organisational policies, formal delegations, committee mandates, approval structures, risk ownership records or statements made by participants.

Where information is incomplete, inconsistent or insufficient, that limitation must be recorded. The resulting classification is not proof of facts that have not been established.

The Diagnostic does not constitute an assurance conclusion, formal governance determination, legal opinion, regulatory determination or individual advisory opinion from TopLink.

The organisation retains responsibility for its decisions, risk acceptance, authority and accountability.
